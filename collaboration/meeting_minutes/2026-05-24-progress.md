- Student D finalised the clustering business use case as product portfolio segmentation for margin-aware promotion decision support.
- Student D confirmed the modelling grain as one row per sold product, with 254 sold products included in the final modelling dataset.
- Student D used the assigned EDA datasets (`sales_territory`, `special_offer`, `special_offer_product`, `product_cost_history`, and `product_list_price_history`) and justified additional modelling tables (`sales_order_detail`, `product`, `product_sub_category`, and `product_category`).

- Student D prepared final clustering inputs using 13 scaled numeric product-level features across demand, revenue, realised price, gross margin, promotion eligibility, actual promotion usage, and historical price/cost movement.
- Student D confirmed that profile fields such as product name, category, subcategory, product line, class, style, and colour are retained for interpretation only and are not used as clustering inputs.
- Student D compared K-Means and Mean Shift. K-Means with `k=5` was selected because it produced five interpretable and commercially actionable product segments with viable cluster sizes.
- Mean Shift produced competitive internal validation scores but created imbalanced clusters, making it less suitable for portfolio-level decision support.
- Student D's final K-Means `k=5` clusters were interpreted as:
  - Cluster 0: Promotion-risk revenue products.
  - Cluster 1: High-margin frequent sellers.
  - Cluster 2: Low-activity review products.
  - Cluster 3: Price-cost volatility products.
  - Cluster 4: Premium revenue products.
- Student D report section will explain that `k=5` was selected for business actionability and balanced interpretation, not because it had the highest silhouette score.
- Student D ethics section will emphasise that the model uses aggregated product-level commercial data only, with no customer identity or demographic attributes.
- Student D ethics section will also flag that low-activity products should not be automatically removed, because some may serve niche, community, or culturally specific needs that transaction volume alone does not capture.
